01
Lane one

Music

Heavy, honest, and finished.

WPAI makes heavy music — metal, industrial, and experimental adjacent. Every track is written, produced, and mixed in-house. AI handles drafts and arrangement experiments; the final sound is directed and signed off by a human ear. Nothing ships that doesn't hit.

The music lane is the oldest part of the studio and the financial foundation. Releases go on streaming. Licensing is open to sync and placement inquiries. The catalog grows slowly because quality gates are real.

Lane two

Software

Tools I built for myself. Now on sale.

Every tool in the software lane started as something I needed and couldn't find. RepoForge Pro bootstraps Python projects the right way. The Mixin Field Manual documents what actually happens when 300 mods share a method. Both are finished, documented, and on the storefront.

The software lane ships developer tools, technical guides, and utilities. The bar is: does it solve a real problem, is it done, and is the documentation honest? If yes, it ships. The free tiers exist because I believe in letting people evaluate before they pay.

How the studio works

One person. Three lanes. Everything ships.

WPAI is a one-person studio. That means every decision — creative direction, quality gate, release timing, pricing — is made by one person with full context and no committee. It also means the output is slower than a team and more coherent than most. The neurodivergence is the engine: hyperfocus on one lane at a time, pattern recognition across all three, and an allergy to shipping things that aren't done.

01

Build for real problems

Every product starts as something I needed. If I had to solve it, someone else does too.

02

Finish before shipping

Nothing goes on the storefront half-done. The quality gate is real and it's mine.

03

Disclose the AI

AI involvement is visible on every release. The collaboration is open. The human authorship is real.

04

Let the work compound

Music funds software. Software funds research. Research feeds everything. The lanes aren't separate — they're one system.
